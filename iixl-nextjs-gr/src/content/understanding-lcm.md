## Understanding LCM (Least Common Multiple)
## 1. Important Terms to Know

Before we start, let's look at the "ingredients" of LCM:

* **Multiple:** The "Table" of a number. (Multiples of 2 are 2, 4, 6, 8...)
* **Common Multiple:** A number that appears in the tables of **both** numbers.
* **Least:** The smallest or the first one.

**So, LCM is simply the SMALLEST number that is a multiple of two or more numbers.**

---

## 2. How to find LCM (Step-by-Step)

There are two main ways for a Class 5 student to find the LCM.

### Method A: The Listing Method (Best for small numbers)
**Problem: Find the LCM of 4 and 6.**

1.  **List Multiples of 4:** 4, 8, **12**, 16, 20, **24**, 28...
2.  **List Multiples of 6:** 6, **12**, 18, **24**, 30...
3.  **Find Common Multiples:** 12 and 24 are both common.
4.  **Pick the Least (Smallest):** The first common number is **12**.

**Answer: LCM of 4 and 6 is 12.**

---

### Method B: The Division Method (Best for larger numbers)
This is like a "Ladder." We divide the numbers together using prime numbers (2, 3, 5, 7...).

**Problem: Find the LCM of 12 and 15.**



| Step | Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Step 1** | Write 12 and 15 in a row. |
| **Step 2** | Divide by **2**: 12 becomes 6. (15 can't be divided by 2, so just write it down again). |
| **Step 3** | Divide by **2** again: 6 becomes 3. (15 stays 15). |
| **Step 4** | Divide by **3**: 3 becomes 1 and 15 becomes 5. |
| **Step 5** | Divide by **5**: 5 becomes 1. Now both numbers are 1! |

**Now, multiply all the numbers on the outside:**
$$2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 5 = \mathbf{60}$$
**Answer: LCM of 12 and 15 is 60.**

---

## 3. Practice Examples

### Example 1: Three Numbers
**Find the LCM of 2, 3, and 5.**
* Multiples of 2: 2, 4, 6... 28, **30**
* Multiples of 3: 3, 6, 9... 27, **30**
* Multiples of 5: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, **30**
* **LCM = 30**

### Example 2: One number is a multiple of the other
**Find the LCM of 5 and 20.**
* Multiples of 5: 5, 10, 15, **20**, 25...
* Multiples of 20: **20**, 40, 60...
* **LCM = 20**
* *Rule: If the bigger number is in the table of the smaller number, the bigger number IS the LCM!*

## 1. The 3 Golden Rules of LCM
Before solving, remember these "shortcuts" used in competitive exams:

* **Rule 1: The Product Rule**
    For any two numbers $A$ and $B$:
    $$HCF \times LCM = A \times B$$
    *(If you know three of these, you can find the fourth!)*
* **Rule 2: The "Together Again" Rule**
    If events happen at different intervals (like bells ringing), the time they happen together is the LCM of those intervals.
* **Rule 3: Fractions LCM**
    To find the LCM of fractions like $\frac{1}{2}$ and $\frac{3}{4}$:
    $$LCM = \frac{\text{LCM of Numerators}}{\text{HCF of Denominators}}$$

## 2. Competitive Question Types & Explanations

### Type 1: The "Bells and Traffic Lights" Problem
**Q: Three bells ring at intervals of 12, 15, and 18 minutes. If they ring together at 9:00 AM, at what time will they next ring together?**

**How to solve:**
1.  **Identify the need for LCM:** Since we want to find when they "overlap" again, find the LCM of 12, 15, and 18.
2.  **Calculate:**
    * Multiples of 12: 12, 24, 36... 
    * Using Division Method: 
        * $2 \mid 12, 15, 18$
        * $2 \mid 6, 15, 9$
        * $3 \mid 3, 15, 9$
        * $3 \mid 1, 5, 3$
        * $5 \mid 1, 5, 1$
        * $LCM = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 5 = \mathbf{180 \text{ minutes}}$.
3.  **Convert to Hours:** 180 minutes = 3 hours.
4.  **Final Time:** 9:00 AM + 3 hours = **12:00 PM**.



---

### Type 2: The "Remainder" Logic
**Q: Find the smallest number which when divided by 24, 36, and 54 leaves a remainder of 5 in each case.**

**How to solve:**
1.  **The Logic:** Find a number that is perfectly divisible by them (LCM), then simply add the remainder.
2.  **Step 1:** Find LCM of 24, 36, and 54.
    * $24 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 3$
    * $36 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3$
    * $54 = 2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3$
    * **LCM = 216**.
3.  **Step 2:** Add the remainder.
    * $216 + 5 = \mathbf{221}$.
**Check:** $221 \div 24$ leaves remainder 5. Correct!

---

### Type 3: The Missing Number (Formula Based)
**Q: The HCF of two numbers is 6 and their LCM is 36. If one number is 12, find the other number.**

**How to solve:**
1.  **Use Rule 1:** $HCF \times LCM = \text{Number 1} \times \text{Number 2}$
2.  **Plug in the values:** $6 \times 36 = 12 \times \text{Number 2}$
3.  **Simplify:** $216 = 12 \times \text{Number 2}$
4.  **Divide:** $\text{Number 2} = 216 \div 12 = \mathbf{18}$.
